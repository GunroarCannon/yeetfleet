# Chains, yeet combat, endless run & performance batch — decisions

| Decision | Choice | Why | Revisit when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Swipe camera zoom limits | `FlightCamera.distance_limits`: a window (× the normal chase distance) the follow views' *whole* distance — pinch × profile pull-out × hit/dash zoom — is clamped into. The swipe pilot sets it every frame from `swipe_cam_min` (0.7) / `swipe_cam_max` (2.6); pinch also stops banking distance past it. All inputs are already eased, so the clamp never snaps | "A max zoom out and max zoom in so the camera does not go too far or too close" | if a crash zoom should still punch past the max — exempt massive hits |
| Swipe look-ahead | The existing `FlightCamera.look_ahead` (slingshot uses it): along the velocity, × the camera distance × `swipe_look_ahead` (0.55), scaled 0.3→1 by speed (cruise → full dash), leaning toward the course-correction / chain target. Pattern: velocity-driven eased look-ahead offset (camera-system skill §2) rather than moving the camera itself | "While moving/dashing the camera should show more of what's ahead so the player can tap to shoot better" | — |
| Kill cam jumping to the target | Root cause: the swoop advanced by real frame time (capped only at 0.1 s). The frames right after a kill are the ones that hitch (explosions, debris, a phone's first-use shader compile), so one or two long frames ate most of the 0.35 s swoop — it looked like a cut to the wreck. The swoop now advances at most 1/30 s per frame (`KILL_CAM_MAX_STEP`). Verified: one 250 ms hitch frame took the old camera 26% of the way in, the new one 3% (`killcam_check`) | "Sometimes it seems like it doesn't tween from player to the ship it destroyed" | — |
| Combo / chains | `ComboChain` (flight root's `combo`): a player kill within `chain_window` (2.5 s, real time) of the last extends the chain. Mobile HUD shows "x3 COMBO!" under the band with a draining bar; each x2+ pops a world shout at the wreck | "A combo number counter (x2 x3, etc.) when ships are destroyed in chains" | — |
| Chain magnet | `MovePilot.chain_bend`: while a chain is live, the swipe *heading itself* turns toward the best enemy within 100° of it (20–220°/s by `chain_magnet`, 0.7), fading with the window, and the pick drifts into the path. Wider than the launch magnet (50°) but only ahead-ish, so a swipe away still escapes | "For chaining to attack other enemies the player should sort of be magnetised towards them" | slingshot could use it on its bounce seek |
| Yeet combat | `YeetDash` per AI ship (like `SlingRun`): IDLE → WINDUP (own red telegraph, `yeet_windup` 0.45 s) → DASH (`external_drive`, `yeet_speed` 2.2 × top speed at the lead point, ≤ 35° off the straight line, 22°/s homing while inbound) → RECOVER → cooldown (`yeet_interval` 3 s). Only hostile, armed ships **in the player's view**, 40–1100 m, and at most `yeet_max_ships` (2) at once. On by default, toggle in 🎮 Controls → AI | "Toggle to make AI more yeet/bash combat focused, on by default, ships that are in view dash at the player" | if too punishing — `yeet_max_ships` 1 or a longer interval |
| Clash rule | In `FlightModel._apply_collision_damage` (player side, yeet on): compare each hull's velocity *into* the other along the line between them, using the velocity written into the body before the step (`pre_impact_velocity`, since impacts are reported after the solver changed them). Player faster → the player takes nothing (their ram still lands); slower → both take damage. A win shouts BAM!/WHAM!/KAPOW! at the contact | "If the player is moving in the direction of them faster the player deals damage and doesn't take any. If slower the player takes damage and also deals its own" | — |
| Lead against a faster player | Found by the check: a dash slower than the player has no intercept, and the lead solver's fallback pointed ships 142° away. Dash speed is at least 1.15 × the player's (capped at 1.6 × its own) and the lead is clamped to 35° | — | — |
| World hit words | `HitWords`: a drawn layer in `FlightHud`, kept in both layouts, that re-projects each word's world point every frame (starburst in the HUD profile's comic style). The mobile HUD's POW!/ZZT! go to `FlightRoot.last_hit_point` (the hit pulled onto the hull) instead of the health panel; `world_hit_words` toggles back | "POW and BAM and WHAM things should be closer to where they happened, not on the HUD" | — |
| Mobile HUD cleanup | The two decorative slabs along the bottom edge are gone. The blue top band is now the guns' charge bar: the capacitor (`SystemsSim.charge_fraction`) fills it in the active weapon's colour with the weapon name on it, pulsing red when a shot can't be afforded. No scenario text; a tap still opens the scenario picker | "Remove bars at the bottom… blue bar = capacity/charge for shooting showing the current weapon, remove text for scenario (but clicking still loads a scenario)" | — |
| Endless run | New module `modules/runner` (façade `EndlessRunner`, one class_name). The ship is pulled down a straight track on the player model's `frame_velocity` (profile-agnostic, never turns the hull), ramping 0.9 → 3.2 × top speed over 100 s; pooled rocks (hit: damage, knock, speed lost), boost rings (+speed) and popcorn fighter waves every ~950 m (which yeet). Death ends the run (best kept), the respawn starts a new one. Speed shows as a meter (`FlightRoot.set_speed_meter`). Reached from the scenario picker ("Endless run") and `runner.tscn` (module registry) | "Another mode/module to test, uses the same UI, set up as an endless runner; speed in a meter" | when it needs real obstacles — a public rock-spawn API on SpaceScene instead of its own pool |
| Performance: profile first | Measured a 12-ship fight (desktop): ~3.5 M triangles and ~490 draw calls a frame, 12.6 ms CPU with no rendering. **96% of the triangles were the drift motes** (`DriftField`): 729 `SphereMesh`es at the default 64×32 segments (4,224 tris each) for a 0.55 m speck. Now 8×4. CPU: the AI think was the largest script cost (spawner ~1.5 ms/frame, `AiBehaviour.update` ~95 µs/ship) and ran every *render* frame | "A lot of battery drain and lag on older devices" | re-profile on the phone after this lands |
| Performance: AI think | `AiSpawner._think`: at most 60 thinks per real second (it was every frame — 2× on a 120 Hz phone); ships > 1200 m away *and* off screen think every 3rd round on their accumulated delta. Kept in `_process` so the checks that pause AI with `set_process(false)` still work | — | — |
| Performance: battery saver | `PerformanceSettings.battery_saver` (new field, so it reaches saved settings; ⚡ panel): on phones caps at 60 fps and the 3D render scale at 80% | A 90/120 Hz phone screen renders the whole scene twice as often for the same game | — |

## Follow-up (same day): playtest round 2

| Decision | Choice | Why | Revisit when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Camera moving without the mouse held (PC) | A button released over a HUD control is eaten by the GUI, so `GestureReader` thought it was still held and every mouse move swung the camera. Mouse motion now carries the truth: its `button_mask` clears a stale left (as a release) or right (alt-drag) button | "Looking around just happens without me holding down the mouse/trackpad" | — |
| Kill cam on ships "not destroyed" + big ships vanishing | With mesh baking on, a hull is one baked mesh with every part's mesh hidden; death scattered the invisible parts and left the whole baked hull. Death (and a part breaking off the player's baked hull) now un-bakes first | "Zoom in goes on ships that are not destroyed", "big ships just disappear instead of breaking apart" | — |
| Emoji, no explosion | A death blast was culled when the ship was off screen at that instant; the kill cam then swooped onto just the dizzy emote. Big (≥1.5) priority blasts are only culled by distance (1.5 km) | "Sometimes I just see the emojis instead of them WITH explosions" | — |
| Debris fade | `DebrisRegistry.retire`: fade (`GeometryInstance3D.transparency`) + shrink over 0.6 s, for the lifetime end, the cap and the off-screen cull | "Parts being removed should tween out in opacity" | — |
| Burst particles | The pooled bursts were flat world-aligned quads (camera-facing was commented out) with over-bright colours: white slivers and squares. New `burst_glow` / `burst_chunk` shaders face the camera and stretch along the on-screen velocity (INSTANCE_CUSTOM); sparks are hot-cored streaks, debris jagged hot-to-cool shards, vapour soft puffs | "White squares, which is bad" (screenshot) | — |
| Outline against blasts | Inverted-hull `material_overlay` on the player's parts only (`hull_outline.gdshader`: fixed pixel width, pushed back from the camera so part shells don't cover neighbours), attached only while a recent big blast is behind the ship on screen. Rejected a screen-space edge outline (no normal buffer on Compatibility, full-screen cost every frame) | "If player ship is behind big white explosion give them an efficient black outline" | — |
| Dynamic camera limits | The swipe window now also bounds the slingshot's dynamic camera, and counts FOV widening as distance (the limit is on how small the ship looks). Dials: 🎮 Controls → "Camera gestures & dynamic camera" | "Dynamic camera does not go too far … player appears very tiny" | — |
| Pre-launch look-ahead | Swipe: while lining up a swipe the camera eases toward the launch direction (further for a longer pull). Slingshot aiming leans toward the magnet's pick and starts at 35% | "As player pulls or gets ready to swipe the camera should … look to where player would be launched" | — |
| Yeet not seen | It only ever targeted the player; in scenarios enemies target the wingmen. Any hostile ship in view now yeets at its own target; "YEET!" pops at launch (`AiSpawner.ship_yeeted`); passive enemies don't yeet | "I do not see yeeting enemy behaviour" | — |
| Planet pinball | `FlightRoot.planet_ram` (called from the player's collision damage): at/above `planet_smash_speed` (×√(radius/1 km), ÷ hull toughness clamped 0.5–2.5) the planet shatters via the weapons' path and the hull is spared; above `planet_knock_speed` it is knocked off its spot (collider + gravity well move) and springs home | "A durable enough ship moving fast enough should be able to yeet and destroy a planet or cause it to move out of line like a pinball" | tune the dials in 🎮 Controls |
| Missions | `ScenarioObjectives` (flight): courier (3 drops), ring race, salvage, dock with a giant carrier, escort a hauler, escort a capital ship (both driven along a route while waves attack), carrier assault (mothership). Blue 3D chevron pointer + ring-and-beam beacons | "Escort and deliver to X destination with a pointer … not directly combat focused … very large ships" | — |
| Mobile HUD | Band fill clipped against the panel (was painted over its corner) and filled sideways from a fixed corner order (the inset polygon's vertex order wasn't); halftone dots kept inside their panel; short weapon names (`short`); no HOLD TO FLY; speed is always a meter under the big number (number font kept); radar bottom-left on swipe/slingshot; portrait puts the speed under the panel; throttle dial hidden for one-thumb profiles | Screenshots + "KMH should be in a meter but keep the font", "radar should show for swipe profiles, look good on portrait" | — |
| Portrait on PC | F2 flips the window between 1280×720 and 540×960 | "What's an easy way I can test portrait view on PC" | — |
| Enemy shot cap leak | Found while chasing a flaky check: the live-shot count was cached per render frame, so catch-up physics steps all fired against a stale total (cap 20 let 200 by). The per-shot gate now refreshes the count and adds each shot it passes | — | — |

## Measured
- Before: 3.21 M triangles visible in the flight scene (DriftField 3.08 M), 42 ms/frame windowed on the dev PC.
- After: ~0.17 M triangles a frame (−95%). Headless CPU, same machine back to back: original ~8.5 ms/frame, this batch with yeet off ~8.2 ms, with yeet on ~11 ms (the fights are busier: more rams, impacts and effects). Windowed frame time on the dev PC was noisy between runs (random fleets, saved HUD state) and isn't a reliable comparison.
