# 20 Gameplay Ideas — Project Nebula

The ship builder and flight model are strong foundations. Here are 20 ideas to make the game deep, fun, and unique — leaning into procedural generation to avoid planet landing while keeping the content infinite.

---

## 1. Procedural Economy + Trading
Each station has supply/demand curves for commodities (fuel cells, ore, weapons, contraband). Prices shift based on what players sell and what stations need. Buy low at a mining station, sell high at a warfront. The economy is emergent, not scripted — every playthrough's market is different.

## 2. Bounty Board
AI ships have bounties (generated from faction reputation and crime level). Scan a ship to see its bounty, accept the contract at a station, then hunt it down. Higher bounties = harder ships with better AI. Bounties scale with system danger level.

## 3. Salvage Rights
When you destroy an AI ship, debris floats with lootable cargo pods and salvageable parts. Fly close to collect. Rare parts from destroyed ships become trade goods or upgrades. Creates a risk/reward loop: fight for loot, or trade for profit.

## 4. Time-Limited Cargo Runs
Pick up a cargo contract at a station: deliver X tonnes of Y to station Z within T seconds. Faster delivery = higher payout. Creates urgency, forces route planning through the belt, and the asteroid field becomes an obstacle course.

## 5. Smuggling
Some cargo is contraband. Stations have scan zones — if you're carrying illegal goods when scanned, you lose faction reputation and pay a fine. Dock at a back-alley station to sell contraband at 3x price. Risk/reward: high profit, high danger.

## 6. Deep Scan Anomalies
Strange signals appear on sensors at random locations. Fly to the signal, deploy a scanner (takes 10 seconds of hovering), and discover: derelict ships with loot, ancient alien artifacts, mineral-rich asteroid clusters, or trapped miners who reward you for rescue.

## 7. Wave Defense
A station is under attack. Accept the defense contract and survive waves of increasingly hostile AI ships. Each wave adds more ships, faster ships, or ships with better weapons. Payout scales with waves survived.

## 8. Asteroid Belt Race
A race course through the belt — fly through checkpoint rings at speed. Fastest time wins credits. Courses are procedurally generated each day. Leaderboards on the station. The belt's tumbling rocks become obstacles, not scenery.

## 9. Escort Missions
A slow cargo hauler needs to reach a station. AI pirates will attack en route. Protect the hauler — if it dies, you lose the payout. Creates dynamic combat encounters where you're defending, not attacking.

## 10. Asteroid Mining
Shoot an asteroid to break it into smaller chunks. Collect the chunks for ore. Different asteroid types yield different materials. Sell at refineries. Creates a resource gathering loop without needing planet surfaces.

## 11. Distress Calls
Random AI ships send distress signals: engine failure, pirate attack, structural damage. Fly to the signal, choose to help (repair them, fight off attackers) or ignore. Helping builds faction reputation and sometimes rewards rare parts.

## 12. Archaeological Digs (Procedural Lore)
Ancient alien structures appear as landmarks in deep space. Scan them to unlock lore entries and technology blueprints. Each structure has a unique procedural puzzle (align energy beams, decode signals) that rewards permanent ship upgrades.

## 13. Faction Standing
Every action affects your reputation with the 6 factions. High standing unlocks: cheaper parts, exclusive ship designs, faction-specific missions, access to restricted stations. Low standing: hostile AI ships, denied docking, bounty hunters after you.

## 14. Rare Commodity Hunts
Some commodities are extremely rare (alien artifacts, prototype weapons, experimental fuel). They appear at random stations at random times. The first player to buy them gets them. Creates a treasure hunt dynamic — check stations frequently.

## 15. Ship Module Upgrades
Instead of just swapping parts, some parts can be upgraded at engineering stations: better weapon damage, faster engines, stronger shields. Upgrades cost credits + materials (from mining/salvage). Creates a long-term progression loop.

## 16. Assassination Contracts
A high-value target is traveling between stations in a powerful ship. Track their route, intercept them, and destroy them. The target has escorts, countermeasures, and sometimes flees. High risk, massive payout.

## 17. Derelict Exploration
Procedurally generated derelict ships float in deep space. Board them (fly inside), navigate corridors, and recover valuable data/logs. Some are traps (armed defenses activate). Creates exploration content without planet surfaces.

## 18. Capital Ship Command
Late-game: acquire a capital ship (the Keel dreadnought). It's too big to fly yourself — you command it from a bridge view, issuing orders to AI fighter squadrons you've recruited. The ship builder becomes a fleet builder.

## 19. Dynamic Story Missions
A procedural narrative: a faction is building a superweapon. You discover fragments of the plot through scanning, trading, and combat. Each playthrough generates a different story arc with different factions, different betrayals, different outcomes. No two games are the same.

## 20. Ship Showroom
Build a ship in the builder, take a screenshot, share it. Other players can download your design and fly it. A community gallery of the best ships, voted on by players. Creates social engagement without multiplayer infrastructure.

---

## Why These Work Together

The key insight: **every system feeds every other system**.

- Mining → materials for upgrades → better ship → harder bounties → more credits → rare parts
- Trading → credits → upgrades → combat → salvage → trading materials
- Faction standing → access → missions → standing → access

The procedural economy ensures no two playthroughs feel the same. The AI ships give every system a reason to exist — they're trading partners, combat targets, escort clients, and lore delivery mechanisms. The ship builder gives every system a reason to care about parts — you're not just collecting stats, you're building the ship that does the thing.

**No planet landing needed.** Every activity happens in space: at stations (dock → menu → undock), in the belt (mining, racing, combat), at landmarks (scanning, archaeology), or in deep space (derelicts, escorts). The station is the "hub" — you fly to it, dock, and interact through menus. No walking, no surface exploration, no rendering engines that aren't already built.
